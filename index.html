01 · The shift

Assets and liabilities are the start. They are not the job.

A current account and a loan tell you the bank already has a relationship. They do not tell you whether the next product should be credit, collections, or neither.

The familiar view

  • Current account, term loan, cash credit, vehicle loan.
  • Conversation stays on limits, outstanding and collateral.
  • The product is chosen before the business is understood.

The 360° view

  • How each rupee is earned, and when it actually hits the account.
  • What must be paid before that money arrives.
  • The product follows the gap — not the other way around.

05 · RM playbook

A sequence, not a product list.

Stay fluent in the two pillars. Then leave them. Tick a question only after you could ask it without looking.

Liabilities

Money the customer keeps with the bank.

  • Operating current account
  • Balances that can sit in a deposit in a strong month
  • Salary accounts for 50+ employees

Assets

Money the bank has lent.

  • Term loan — ovens, interiors, a second outlet
  • Cash credit — the gap between payouts and collections
  • Vehicle loan — refrigerated or delivery transport

Leave with this

The product follows the cycle.

  • Ask how the business earns, when cash lands, and what must be paid first.
  • Use loans and deposits when they match that cycle. Do not lead with them.
  • One understood customer is a better session than a catalogue of products.

Training case for relationship managers. Offer only what fits eligibility, policy and repayment capacity.
